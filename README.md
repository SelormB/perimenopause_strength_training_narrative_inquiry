# The Weight of the Room

## A Narrative Inquiry into the Perceived Culture of Strength Training Spaces Among Perimenopausal Women

## Overview

This qualitative research project explores how perimenopausal women who participate in strength training perceive and experience the culture of their workout spaces.

Perimenopause can involve substantial physical and psychological changes that affect women's relationships with their bodies, identity, and physical activity. At the same time, strength-training environments may contain social expectations, gender norms, sensory characteristics, and other features that influence women's experiences.

Using narrative inquiry, this study examined how women described navigating these environments during the perimenopausal transition.

## Research Question

The central research question was:

**How do perimenopausal women who strength train perceive the culture of the workout spaces they occupy?**

The study sought to understand how workout environments may act as either motivators or barriers as women navigate physical and psychological changes associated with perimenopause.

## Methodology

The study used a **qualitative narrative inquiry** design informed by **Social Cognitive Theory**.

### Participants

Seven women currently experiencing the perimenopausal transition and participating in strength training were interviewed.

Participants represented different strength-training experiences and workout environments.

### Data Collection

Data were collected primarily through **semi-structured interviews**.

The interview guide explored three broad areas:

1. Entry into strength training and workout spaces
2. Perimenopause and physical agency
3. Gender expression and the social architecture of workout environments

Interviews were designed to encourage participants to describe their experiences, perceptions, and stories in their own words.

## Qualitative Analysis

Interview transcripts were analyzed using **narrative thematic analysis**.

The analysis involved:

- Reviewing interview transcripts
- Developing qualitative codes
- Identifying recurring patterns and themes
- Comparing interpretations across researchers
- Examining both shared and differing participant experiences

To strengthen trustworthiness, the research team used multiple forms of **triangulation**. Researchers independently analyzed interviews, reviewed interviews conducted by other team members, compared findings, and collaboratively discussed coding and theme development.

## Major Themes

Five recurring themes emerged from the interviews.

### 1. Navigating Changing Bodies and Invisibility

Participants described how physical and emotional changes associated with perimenopause influenced body awareness, motivation, and their experiences within workout environments.

### 2. Social Belonging: The Spectrum of Tribe and Solitude

Participants differed in the type of social environment they preferred. Some valued the community and belonging created through strength training, while others valued autonomy, privacy, and the ability to exercise without feeling observed.

### 3. From Aesthetic "Shrinking" to Functional Longevity

Many participants described a shift in their reasons for strength training.

Rather than focusing primarily on appearance, participants increasingly emphasized strength, mobility, independence, physical capability, and maintaining function as they age.

### 4. Environmental Sensitivities as Cultural Signals

Participants described cleanliness, equipment maintenance, crowding, temperature, music, and other characteristics of workout spaces as meaningful components of gym culture.

These environmental characteristics could either support or interfere with their workout experiences.

### 5. Reclaiming the Weight Floor and Agency

Strength training was frequently described as a source of empowerment and personal agency.

Developing technical knowledge, becoming comfortable with equipment, and seeing other women participating in strength training helped some participants feel greater ownership and confidence within traditionally male-dominated weight-training spaces.

## Key Implications

The findings suggest that inclusive strength-training environments for midlife women may benefit from:

- Emphasizing functional longevity and physical capability
- Providing opportunities for both social connection and individual training
- Maintaining clean, organized, and comfortable workout environments
- Improving access to technical instruction and mentorship
- Creating environments where women can develop confidence and ownership of strength-training spaces

## Ethical Considerations

Participant confidentiality was maintained throughout the study. Identifying information was replaced with pseudonyms, and recordings and transcripts were stored in password-protected files accessible only to the research team.

Raw interview recordings, transcripts, participant-level demographic information, and other potentially identifying research materials are **not included in this public repository**.

## Limitations

The study included seven women who were already participating in strength training, meaning the experiences of women who avoid or discontinue strength training were not represented.

Most participants were also geographically concentrated in northwestern Colorado. As a qualitative study, the findings are intended to provide depth and insight into participants' experiences rather than statistical generalization to all perimenopausal women.
